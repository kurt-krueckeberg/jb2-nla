# Reading a Case File: Contents List, Numbers, and Notations

The documents in a Rentkammer case file carry many small numbers and notes
in their margins. They were written by clerks of the Rentkammer and of the
local Amt as each paper arrived, was answered, and was filed. Once you know
what they mean, they show the path each paper took. This page explains the
most common ones, using case file 689 (Krückeberg's loan, 1841–1845) as the
main example.

## The Designatio Actorum: the case file's table of contents

The **Designatio Actorum** (also *Acten-Designation*) is the table of contents
of the case file. A clerk wrote it as a numbered list with brief titles. Each
numbered entry can contain more than one document; a ditto mark (〃) means
the document belongs to the entry above it. In case file 689, for example,
the first three entries are:

```{list-table}

* - Number
  - Documents
* - 1.
  - [Vorstellung des Col. Krückeberg No 10 in Berenbusch------Anb.](../689/doc1.md)
* - 2.
  - [Bericht Amts Bückeburg](../689/doc2-1.md)
* - 〃
  - [Cpt. KRes. an dasselbe](../689/doc2-2.md)
* - 3.
  - [Vorst. des Colons Krückeberg](../689/doc3-1.md)
* - 〃
  - [Vortrag des GKR od Reck](../689/doc3-2.md)
* - 〃
  - [Cpt. KRes. an p Krückeberg](../689/doc3-3.md)
```

The first document of each entry has the entry's number written in its upper
right corner. The first document of entry **2**, the
[Bericht Amts Bückeburg](../689/doc2-1.md) (report of the Bückeburg office),

## Two offices, two sets of numbers

A paper in these case files was often handled by two offices: the Rentkammer
in Bückeburg and the local Amt Bückeburg. Each office kept its own record of
the papers it received and gave each paper its own running number. This was
normal practice in German offices of the time: an incoming letter was marked
with the date it was received (the *praesentatum*) and entered in the office's
journal under a running number, which tied together the papers of one
matter.[^aktenkunde]

That is why one paper can carry two numbers: the **NC** number of the
Rentkammer and the **Nr pr** number of the Amt.

### NC: the Rentkammer's number

```{figure} ../689/images/nc-xxxx.jpg
:class: image-override
```

The **NC** number is the number the Rentkammer gave a paper when it arrived.
It is written in many ways: *N.C.*, *NC*, *N. Cam.*, *Num: Cam:*, or
*Cam: N:*, all meaning "Kammer number". It usually stands in the upper left
corner of the first page, often beside the date of arrival, as in
"NC 4381 pr. in Cam 2 Oct 1841": number 4381, presented in the Kammer on
2 October 1841.[^nc4381] The image above shows NC 5587, the number given to
Officer Creuzinger's report when it arrived on 11 December 1841.[^nc5587]

Each paper that arrived got its own NC number; it is not a number for the
whole case file. The papers of case file 689 received NC 4381, 4487, 5038,
5587 and 5702 in 1841 alone. The Rentkammer's own draft reply was filed
under the number of the paper it answered, so it carries that number, either
alone or after "ad" (see below).

The count started over every January. The prince's order of 19 December 1841
is NC 5702, and the Rentkammer's draft order of 24 December 1841, written in
reply to it, carries the same number. The Amt's answer to that order,
received on 17 January 1842, is NC 274.[^newyear] The same pattern appears in
the other case files: a
report received on 15 January 1809 is NC 15, while one received on
8 November 1809 is NC 368.[^yr1809]

### Nr pr: the Amt's number

```{figure} ../689/images/nr-pr.jpg
:class: image-override
```

The **Nr pr** number (also *Num. Pr.*, *No. pr.*, *N. pr.*) is the number
under which the **Amt Bückeburg** registered a paper when it was presented
there. "pr." is short for *praesentatum*, "presented". The image above shows
"Nr. pr: 1274" at the foot of the first page of Krückeberg's petition of
2 October 1841.

Two petitions to sell show the two numbers side by side. Carl Friedrich
Krückeberg's request of 1850 is marked "No. pr. 1314, pr. bei Amte 12/9 50"
(received at the Amt on 12 September 1850 as its number 1314) and also
"N.C. 4386, pr. in Cam. 17/9. 50" (received at the Rentkammer five days later
as its number 4386).[^twonumbers] The Amt's count also started over each
year: an Amt report of February 1842 carries Num. Pr. 116.[^amt116]

The petition of 1841 shows how a paper could collect both numbers. It reached
the Rentkammer first and became NC 4381. The Rentkammer sent it to the Amt
for a report, and the Amt registered it as its number 1274 and wrote that
number on it. The Amt's report then begins "Num Pr. 1274 ad N. C. 4381":
"our number 1274, concerning your number 4381".[^amt1274]

### "ad": in reply to

```{figure} ../689/images/ad-nc.jpg
:class: image-override
```

**ad** means "concerning" or "in reply to". A number after "ad" is the number
of the earlier paper being answered, not the number of the paper it is
written on. The image above shows "ad Num: Cam: 5702" on the Amt's report of
12 January 1842, which answers the Rentkammer's order filed under number
5702.[^newyear]

The Rentkammer used "ad" the same way. Its draft reply to the Amt report
numbered Cam: N: 53 in case file 1237 is headed "ad nr: Cam: 53".[^cf1237] It
could also cite the Amt's number: a Rentkammer draft of 1818 is headed
"ad Num: Cam: 101" and "ad Num. Praes. 831", referring to an Amt report that
the Rentkammer received as its number 101 and that carries the Amt's
"No. pr. 831".[^cf1291]

## Other notes you will see

Concept (Conc., Cpt.)
: The draft of a letter or decision written in the Rentkammer. The fair copy
  was sent; the draft stayed in the case file. Most Rentkammer orders in these
  case files are therefore drafts.[^aktenkunde]

Exp. p. [name]
: *Expedirt per*: the clerk named made the fair copy and dispatched it.[^aktenkunde]

Res: Cam:
: The Rentkammer's decision (*Resolution der Cammer*), written directly on the
  incoming paper.

ad acta
: "To the file": no further action; the paper was filed.[^aktenkunde]
