# What Was the Rentkammer?

The Rentkammer (literally "revenue chamber") was the office in Bückeburg that managed
the property and income of the ruling Count, later Prince, of Schaumburg-Lippe. When a
Krückeberg wanted a larger garden, a piece of forest land, a new building site, a loan,
or permission to sell, he had to ask the Rentkammer, because the land and the dues
attached to it ultimately belonged to the ruler.

## One of three central offices

Schaumburg-Lippe was created in 1647 when the old County of Schaumburg was divided. At
first a single government office (the *Regierung*) in Bückeburg handled everything.
Finance was split off into a separate Rentkammer around 1700 and finally in 1728, and
legal matters were reserved to a separate *Justizkanzlei* from 1765.[^l2]
From then on there were three central bodies:

- the **Regierung** (government), for sovereign and police matters,
- the **Justizkanzlei**, for law and courts,
- the **Rentkammer**, for the ruler's property and revenue.

The archivists note that the boundaries between these offices were never perfectly
sharp.[^l2b]

## What the Rentkammer managed

Around 1700 the administration of the ruler's property was separated from his sovereign
and police powers. The Rentkammer took over the princely farms (*Vorwerke*), the labour
services owed by the subjects (*Dienste*), and the allotment of land (*Landanweisungen*).[^k1] It administered the house and chamber estates (*Haus- und Kammergüter*),
including estates outside the country, whose income flowed into the chamber treasury
(*Kammerkasse*).[^k2]

Because the state budget and the ruler's own estate budget were not kept apart, the
Rentkammer was in effect the central treasury of the country.[^k1b] The ruler
himself presided over it; Prince Georg Wilhelm (reigned 1807–1860) did so personally.[^mey]

Its members were senior officials with the title *Kammerrat*. Ernst Iffland, for
example, was appointed Kammerrat at the "Fürstliche Rentkammer zu Bückeburg" in 1853.[^dep]

## Why the Krückebergs dealt with the Rentkammer

The Krückeberg holdings in Berenbusch were held under the Kolonat (see
[Stätte, Kolon, and Kolonat](staette-kolonat-kolon.md)). Enlarging a garden, receiving
forest land, founding a new holding, borrowing money, or selling a holding all touched
land, services, or dues that belonged to the ruler. These were therefore Rentkammer
matters. The Rentkammer did not act alone: it asked the local *Amt Bückeburg*, and where
forest land was concerned the forestry officials, for reports before deciding. How that
worked is described in [How a Case Moved Through the Rentkammer](workflow.md).

## Where the records are today

The Rentkammer's records are kept in the Lower Saxony State Archive in Bückeburg as the
archive of the princely *Hofkammer*: the older chamber archive (NLA BU K 1, 1647–1777)
and the younger chamber archive (NLA BU K 2, 1777–1918).[^kk] This
is why most case files in this book have call numbers beginning "NLA BU, K 2".
